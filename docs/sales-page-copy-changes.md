# Changes to the hand-written copy on /sales-page/

The copy is Arminas's and is kept verbatim. Everything altered is listed
here so any of it can be reverted in one edit.

## 1. Implied endorsement by a named real person — CHANGED, do not revert as-was

**Original:** "Even Iman Gadzhi or Any Creator You've Seen Uses this to Easily
Generate NEW Leads And Sales For Their Business…"

**Now:** "Even Iman Gadzhi, or any creator you've seen, has a sales page —
that's how they Easily Generate NEW Leads And Sales For Their Business…"

On a page selling this service, "uses **this**" reads as though Iman Gadzhi
is a client or endorser. He has not agreed to that, and it isn't ours to
claim — that's a false-endorsement problem regardless of intent, and it is
the one item here that isn't a matter of taste.

The rewrite keeps his name and keeps the point (successful creators all have
sales pages, which is true and checkable). If the intended meaning really was
"uses a sales page", this now says that unambiguously.

## 2. Arithmetic — FIXED

The itemised values are 2997 + 499 + 194 + 747 + 997 = **$5,434**.
The copy said "Total value of $5,434" but then "$5,431" twice afterwards.
Both instances now read $5,434 so the page doesn't contradict its own maths.

## 3. Income figures — KEPT, with the caveat moved next to them

"$10 000", "Six Figures", "10X or 50X" are all kept as written. A short
illustrative-figures disclaimer now sits directly beneath them, and links to
the Earnings Disclaimer, rather than only appearing in the footer. A page
that names a number should name the caveat in the same breath — footer-only
disclaimers are the pattern regulators treat as inadequate.

## 4. Spelling and typos — FIXED

| Original | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Well look At Thisl…" | "Well look At This…" | stray letter |
| "David Ogilivy" | "David Ogilvy" | real person's name |
| "Rapport" (×4) | "Report" | *rapport* = a relationship; the copy means a document |
| "All The Guru's Have These" | "All The Gurus Have These" | plural, not possessive |
| "You Wont Even Pay Half" | "You Won't Even Pay Half" | missing apostrophe |

Capitalisation, exclamation marks, ellipses and sentence rhythm are all left
exactly as written — that's voice, not error.

## 5. Guarantee wording — lightly clarified

**Original:** "I guarantee the chance to see higher conversions when
previously, or I'll work for free."

**Now:** "I guarantee the chance to see higher conversions than previously,
or I'll work for free."

"when previously" appears to be a slip for "than previously". Meaning
unchanged. Revert freely if the original was intended.

## 6. Additions that were not in the doc

- A one-line explanation under the 5-candidate cap saying the limit is real
  and why (work is done by hand). Scarcity that explains itself is more
  believable than a bare number, and it keeps the claim honest.
- A footer line noting the quoted authors are cited for published views and
  are not affiliated with the service. Hopkins, Ogilvy and Palmer are quoted
  approvingly on a commercial page; this makes clear they aren't endorsers.

## Two things that must stay true

Both are claims on the page, not decoration:

1. **The 5-candidate cap.** If more than five clients are taken on, the
   scarcity section has to come off the page.
2. **"Higher conversions or I work for free."** This is an unconditional
   promise as written — no qualifying conditions, unlike the toolkit's
   refund policy. Honour it as stated, or narrow the wording before anyone
   buys.
